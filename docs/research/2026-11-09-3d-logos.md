# 3D framework logos for the Champion Select stack

Date: 2026-11-09. Reader: the front-end dev building the "Stack Select" section (`champ-select.md` §5) on the WebGL stage in `webgl-magic.md`. This report covers turning real framework logos into 3D meshes. It does not repeat the pedestal, rings, LOCK IN timeline, version matrix or stage architecture from those docs; it corrects them where measurements disagree (§8).

Tags: **[measured]** I ran it today (method in §9). **[src]** read in published package source. **[doc]** official docs, issue tracker or brand page. **[proposal]** starting value, tune by eye. **UNVERIFIED** could not confirm.

Every visual claim below was rendered and inspected, on three r187 and, where noted, r171 (the repo's version).

## 0. Decisions

1. **Use the Simple Icons 16.37.0 paths, not `public/icons/*.svg`, for 3D.** Copy the 11 path strings into `src/data/logos3d.ts`; that is 10.4 kB raw (5 kB gzipped) and needs no dependency. 14 of the 20 framework/brand SVGs in `public/icons` need replacing or cleanup before they can be extruded: stacked coplanar layers, gradients, wordmarks, `rx="15%"`, outdated marks (§1).
2. **Java uses OpenJDK's Duke.** Simple Icons dropped the Java cup in v7.0.0 (May 2022) after a removal request from Oracle's attorney (issue #7374), and later added a workflow that auto-closes Java requests. Duke is in Simple Icons as `openjdk`, licence BSD-3-Clause. The Angular icon there is **CC BY 4.0**, so it needs a credit line. [doc]
3. **Pipeline** (verified on r171 and r187):
   - parse, then `toShapes` (`createShapes` before r185)
   - **sanitize** the polygons (drop the near-duplicate closing point and collinear points)
   - `ExtrudeGeometry` with an **inward bevel**: `bevelOffset = -bevelSize`, so the silhouette stays exactly the logo
   - `toCreasedNormals(π/3)` **in SVG units**
   - `mergeGroups`
   - flip and scale on the mesh, not in the geometry

   Cost: 154 ms in total for the 11 unique champion logos (Node, i5-10210U). The worst single logo is Duke at 45 ms; React takes 23 ms. [measured]
4. **Look: enamel and gold.**
   - Face (group 0): `MeshPhysicalMaterial` in the brand colour, **`flatShading: true`**.
   - Sides and bevel (group 1): gold metal `#C89B3C`, metalness 1, roughness 0.28.
   - `flatShading` makes the cap perfectly flat even though its vertex normals are averaged with the bevel. That lets the gold bevel be smooth while the face stays flat. [measured]
5. **Tone mapping: NEUTRAL, not AgX, wherever the logos render.** In a clean A/B, AgX washed out every brand colour (Qt, TypeScript, Supabase, Redis) and turned the gold beige. postprocessing 6.39.6 has `ToneMappingMode.NEUTRAL` (8). [measured][src]
6. **Build at runtime and cache; don't prebuild.** Prebuilt geometry for the 11 champions is 936 kB gzipped as float32, or 691 kB quantized, against 5 kB of path strings. [measured]
7. **Multi-colour only where it pays off:**
   - Kotlin and Angular gradients, by injecting the official gradient into the SVG string. This needs r185 or later for `SVGLoader.createFillMaterial`.
   - Python in two colours, one mesh per shape.
   - Optional three-blue Flutter from the CC0 gilbarbara source.
   - **Bug in r187 and in three.js `dev` today:** a gradient stop `offset="46.89%"` is parsed as 1. Rewrite offsets to fractions first. [measured][src]
8. **Swap between logos with an object-space dissolve:** an `onBeforeCompile` patch on both materials, plus GSAP on L = 600 ms. It compiled and ran on r171 and r187 with GL error 0. [measured]
9. **Lighting and shadow:**
   - `RoomEnvironment` through PMREM, set as `envMap` on the logo materials only. It needs no network.
   - Avoid drei `preset`s: they load HDRs from raw.githack.com, and the docs say not to use them in production. [doc][src]
   - Use a blob shadow, not `ContactShadows`, which re-renders the whole scene every frame. [src]

## 3. Geometry technique

### 3.1 Parse and classify [src]

- **Prefer `shapePath.toShapes()` from r185 on.** `SVGLoader.parse()` returns `ShapePath`s, with the style in `userData.style` (including `fillRule`). From **r185**, `SVGLoader.createShapes()` is deprecated: it logs a warning on every call and forwards to `shapePath.toShapes()`.
  - The fill-rule solver moved into `ShapePath.toShapes()`. It is a port of paper.js `reorientPaths()`: sort sub-paths by |area|, find each one's innermost container by bounding box plus point-in-polygon, accumulate winding, then drop sub-paths that don't flip "insideness".
  - `nonzero` and `evenodd` are supported; other values fall back to `nonzero` with a warning.
  - Its own comment says it is valid only for simple (non-self-intersecting) sub-paths. That is why `vehicool.svg` diverges between versions.
- **r171** has no gradient support and uses the older scanline `createShapes()`. **In r171 `ShapePath.toShapes()` is the old font algorithm: do not call it.**
- **The two versions agree:** r171 `createShapes` and r187 `toShapes` gave identical shape and hole counts on all 28 Simple Icons tested and on 22 of 23 project SVGs. [measured]
- **Path direction doesn't matter.** `ExtrudeGeometry` reverses the outer contour to clockwise and holes to counter-clockwise itself.
- **What landed in r185:** SVGLoader commits after r184 (2026-04-16) and before r185 (2026-06-25):
  - #33415 "Improve createShapes()"
  - #33424 material helpers
  - #33436 basic gradients
  - #33503 `ShapePath.toShapes()`

Version-safe call:

```js
const toShapes = (p) => (Number(THREE.REVISION) >= 185 ? p.toShapes() : SVGLoader.createShapes(p))
```

### 3.2 Sanitize first: Simple Icons paths carry micro-edges [measured]

- **Where the micro-edges come from:**
  - Every React contour ends 0.001–0.002 units short of its start. Simple Icons rounds to 3 decimals, so `extractPoints` emits a near-duplicate closing point and an edge of about 0.001.
  - Arcs (`a` commands) also leave clusters of very close points. NestJS's outer contour has 592 of 2,267 points within 0.01 of the previous one; PostgreSQL has 54/351, MinIO 39/166.
  - `ExtrudeGeometry`'s own `mergeOverlappingPoints` only removes points within about 1e-10 of each other, so these stay.
- **Why it matters:** `getBevelVec` computes a miter from the two neighbouring edges. A 0.001-long edge next to a normal edge gives a wild miter direction, which shows as bevel spikes. Each such point also adds triangles.
- **Fix:** polygonise once at curveSegments 8, drop points closer than 0.01 to their predecessor (the closing point included), drop collinear points (turn under 0.5°), then extrude the polygons with `curveSegments: 1`. Sanitizing costs 0.1–2.5 ms per logo.

|---|---|---|
| React | 12,538 → 10,892 | 26.2 → 23.0 |
| NestJS | 37,084 → 22,174 | 152 → 68 |
| PostgreSQL | 21,066 → 15,702 | 76 → 69 |
| Duke | 9,128 → 7,048 | 42 → 45 (noise) |

### 3.3 Bevel: direction and size [src][measured]

How `ExtrudeGeometry` bevels (r187 source):
- The cap layer sits at offset `bevelOffset`, the side walls at `bevelSize + bevelOffset`.
- The profile is a quarter-circle: `z = bt·cos(t·π/2)`, `offset = bs·sin(t·π/2)`.
- Total thickness is `depth + 2·bevelThickness`.
- Corner miters are clamped to √2·size.

So the default `bevelOffset = 0` **grows the logo outward** by `bevelSize`. That closes gaps (PostgreSQL's `rOut` is 0.024, so it fuses at any visible bevel), shrinks holes, and changes the outline, which brand rules forbid (§2.1). `bevelOffset = -bevelSize` **keeps the outline exact** and shrinks only the coloured face.

Why inward by default:
- **Fidelity:** an outward bevel redraws the outline and fuses narrow gaps (PostgreSQL's `rOut` is 0.024).
- **The real offset algorithm favours inward more often.** I re-implemented `getBevelVec` (copied from r187), offset every contour and looked for the first crossing. Inward survived longer for 11 of 20 logos, outward for 8, with one tie. Outward fails early at sharp concave corners (the S terminals, the petal tips of the React orbits). First crossings, out/in:

  | Logo | Out | In |
  |---|---|---|
  | React | .155 | .534 |
  | TypeScript | .031 | .492 |
  | JavaScript | .017 | .144 |
  | Duke | .020 | .135 |

- **Morphology alone is mixed:** rIn > rOut for 7 logos, rOut > rIn for 9, ties for 4. So go outward only where strokes are too thin for a visible inward bevel and the gaps are wider: C++ (rIn .30, rOut .57) and NestJS (.024, .105).

Rule used for every row in §2.2 [proposal; all 20 rendered and inspected]:
- `bevel = rIn >= 0.3 || rIn >= rOut ? 'in' : 'out'`
- `size = clamp(0.35 · (in ? rIn : rOut), 0.03, 0.25)`
- `bevelThickness = max(0.12, 1.6·size)`
- `depth = 2`, `bevelSegments = 3`

All values are in 24-unit SVG space; the world size is set in §3.6.

The 0.35 factor keeps about 65% of each half-stroke as coloured face. At 0.7 the outline logos (PostgreSQL, Duke, Node) turned almost all gold.

Logos needing a closer look at 2× zoom, where the polygon test reports early local crossings:
- MinIO: in 0.012 against a chosen 0.24.
- PostgreSQL: in 0.114 against 0.12.
- Duke: 0.03.

At champion size the artefacts weren't visible in my renders.

### 3.4 Normals: creased in SVG units, flat-shaded face [src][measured]

- **Default normals band on gold.** `ExtrudeGeometry` is non-indexed and calls `computeVertexNormals()`, which gives **flat** normals: curved walls and the bevel show facet bands. That is very visible on gold with environment reflections (see the naive PostgreSQL close-up).
- **Fix:** `toCreasedNormals(g, Math.PI/3)` from BufferGeometryUtils smooths every bevel and wall step, since all are under 40° for the settings above.
  - It also bends the cap normals at the rim, and every cap vertex is a rim vertex, because earcut adds no interior points.
  - **`flatShading: true` on the face material neutralises that.** The shader derives the normal from `dFdx/dFdy` of the view position, and the cap is planar. The result is a perfectly flat face with a smooth gold bevel.
- **Gotcha: `toCreasedNormals` hashes positions at `Math.trunc(pos·100)`, a 0.01 grid** (r187 source). Run it **before** any downscale. When run after scaling:

  | Logo | Normals off by >5° at 1/12 scale | At 1/24 scale |
  |---|---|---|
  | React | 5.8% | 74.9% (worst 47.7°) |
  | PostgreSQL | 10.7% | 78.0% (worst 167°) |
  | JavaScript | 4.1% | 71.1% |
  | Kotlin | 0% | 43.9% |

- **Versions:** r187's `toCreasedNormals` is a typed-array rewrite, 4–20× faster than r171's (React 3.9 vs 22.7 ms, TypeScript 1.7 vs 36 ms in Node). On r171 the normals step is the largest cost.

### 3.5 Groups and draw calls [src][measured]

- **Each Shape adds its own pair of groups:** `buildLidFaces()` adds a group with `materialIndex` 0 (both caps), and `buildSideFaces()` adds one with `materialIndex` 1 (walls and bevel).
- **A material array then costs 2 draw calls per shape:** React 4, Angular 8, Docker 20.
- **`BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGroups(g)`** (present in r171 and r187) sorts by material, builds an index and collapses them into 2 groups, so 2 draw calls. Verified on React: `[[0,4200,0],[4200,28476,1]]`, 8 calls for 4 meshes.

### 3.6 Orientation, centring, optical size [src][measured]

- **Don't bake the flip; use a mirrored mesh.** `mesh.scale = [k, -k, k]` works: `WebGLRenderer` sets `frontFaceCW` when `matrixWorld.determinantAffine() < 0`, so culling stays correct.
  - Baking `geometry.scale(1,-1,1)` would invert the winding and show back faces.
  - `geometry.rotateX(Math.PI)` is the safe way if you must bake.
  - Leaving the geometry unbaked also skips three passes over about 33k vertices for React.
- **Centre** with `position = [-c.x·k, c.y·k, -c.z·k]`, where `c = geometry.boundingBox.getCenter()` in SVG units.
- **Size:** Simple Icons already fits each icon's longer side to 24, so `k = 2/24` gives 2 world units. Visual weight still varies 6×, from MinIO at 13% coverage to JavaScript at 86%. Multiply `k` by `optical = clamp((0.45 / coverage)^0.25, 0.85, 1.2)` (§2.2 column). [proposal]

### 3.7 Materials and tone mapping [measured]

- **Face (enamel):** `MeshPhysicalMaterial`.
  - `color` = brand hex
  - `flatShading: true`
  - `roughness: .38`, `metalness: .05`
  - `clearcoat: .6`, `clearcoatRoughness: .12`
  - `envMapIntensity: .7`

  My first sheet (clearcoat 1, roughness 0.08) had whiter faces. AgX was on at the same time, so the clearcoat share is not isolated.
- **Sides and bevel (gold):** `MeshStandardMaterial` `#C89B3C`, `metalness: 1`, `roughness: .28`. One shared instance; clone it only when the dissolve needs per-instance uniforms.
- **Near-black brand colours** (Next.js and Duke `#000000`, Angular's `#0F0F11` hex, Expo `#1C2024`): use gold-100 `#F0E6D2` ivory on the hextech-black background.
- **Tone mapping A/B** (same materials and lights, only the tone mapping changed):
  - `NeutralToneMapping` keeps Qt green, TypeScript blue and Supabase mint saturated, and the gold rich.
  - `AgXToneMapping` greys them and turns the gold beige.
  - `NeutralToneMapping` is present in r171 too.
  - If the logos go through the post chain from `webgl-magic.md` §5, use `<ToneMapping mode={ToneMappingMode.NEUTRAL} />`. Otherwise render the stage so it isn't AgX-mapped.
- **Iridescence 1.0 on the gold** (IOR 1.3, thickness 100–400 nm) gives a rose and green anodised look that competes with the brand face. Keep it at 0. A brief 0 → 0.4 → 0 pulse at LOCK IN is possible (taste call, not rendered).
- **Transmission:** skip it on logos. It is a separate full-scene pass ([src]; cost measured in `webgl-magic.md` §3.3) and muddies brand colour.

### 3.8 Multi-colour logos [measured]

Three approaches, all rendered:

1. **Simple Icons geometry plus an injected official gradient** (Kotlin, Angular; r185+).
   - Write `<defs><linearGradient …objectBoundingBox…>` into the SVG string and set `fill="url(#g)"`.
   - Parse it, take `SVGLoader.createFillMaterial(path).map` (a `CanvasTexture` with `texture.matrix` set), and put it in the enamel material's `map` with colour white.
   - `ExtrudeGeometry` cap UVs are the raw shape x and y (`WorldUVGenerator.generateTopUV`), so the cap samples the gradient exactly. The gold sides don't use the map.
   - Rendered: Kotlin purple bottom-left → magenta → coral top-right; Angular red → magenta → violet.
2. **Per-shape colour on the Simple Icons geometry** (Python): `toShapes()` gives 2 shapes. Sort by bounding-box centre y in SVG space: top (smaller y) is blue `#3776AB`, the other is yellow `#FFE052`. Build one geometry per part and mesh them separately.
3. **CC0 gilbarbara files, one mesh per `<path>`** (Flutter 3-tone, C++ relief). Rules that gave clean renders:
   - drop paths with `fillOpacity < 1` or any gradient stop with `opacity < 1` (shadows and sheens)
   - raise each part that sits inside an earlier part by 0.45 SI-units per level
   - use `createFillMaterial(path)` for the colour or texture

**r187 bug:** `parseGradients` does `parseFloat(offset)` and clamps it to [0, 1], so `"46.89%"` becomes 1. Without a fix, Kotlin loses its purple and Angular its violet. The `dev` branch still has the same line (checked 2026-11-09). Workaround, before parsing:

```js
svgText = svgText.replace(/offset="([\d.]+)%"/g, (m, n) => `offset="${n / 100}"`)
```

On r171 there is no gradient support: use flat colours (Kotlin `#7F52FF`, Angular `#E40035`) or upgrade.

### 3.9 Cost, caching, Suspense, workers [measured][doc]

- **Build cost:** the 11 unique champion logos take **154 ms total** in Node (React Native reuses React). Duke is the maximum at **45 ms**, React 23 ms, TypeScript 23 ms. In the embedded Chromium, cold with JIT and the tab hidden, React took 107 ms on r187 and 125 ms on r171; that is an upper bound.
- **Memory** for all 11 cached: 131k vertices, 43.7k triangles, about **4.7 MB** (3.7 MB without the `uv` attribute, if no gradients are used).
- **Cache** in a module-level `Map` keyed by slug, and never dispose. Build synchronously on first selection; that blocks for at most one Duke build. Pre-warm the rest in idle callbacks once the hero is interactive (§5.3).
- **Suspense isn't needed**, since the build is synchronous.
- **Safari:** `requestIdleCallback` is "Limited availability" on MDN, not Baseline, so use a `setTimeout` fallback. Safari's exact status is UNVERIFIED.
- **Workers:** only worth it if NestJS or PostgreSQL go 3D (about 70 ms each). `DOMParser` is unavailable in workers, so parse and sanitize on the main thread (≤ 2.5 ms), post flat `Float32Array`s of the rings, and run `ExtrudeGeometry`, normals and `mergeGroups` in a module worker. Transfer the position, normal and index buffers back. Vite's recommended syntax is `new Worker(new URL('./logoWorker.js', import.meta.url), { type: 'module' })`.

// src/components/Stack/logo3d.ts
import * as THREE from 'three'
import { SVGLoader } from 'three/examples/jsm/loaders/SVGLoader.js'
import { toCreasedNormals, mergeGroups } from 'three/examples/jsm/utils/BufferGeometryUtils.js'

const toShapes = (p) => (Number(THREE.REVISION) >= 185 ? p.toShapes() : SVGLoader.createShapes(p)) // createShapes() warns from r185
const COS_HALF_DEG = Math.cos(Math.PI / 360)

// Simple Icons paths end ~0.001 short of their start; that micro-edge and collinear runs make bevel spikes.
function clean(pts, eps = 0.01) {
  let r = pts.filter((p, i) => p.distanceTo(pts[(i + pts.length - 1) % pts.length]) >= eps)
  return r.filter((p, i) => {
    const a = r[(i + r.length - 1) % r.length], b = r[(i + 1) % r.length]
    const ux = p.x - a.x, uy = p.y - a.y, vx = b.x - p.x, vy = b.y - p.y
    return (ux * vx + uy * vy) / (Math.hypot(ux, uy) * Math.hypot(vx, vy)) < COS_HALF_DEG
  })
}

const cache = new Map()
export function logoGeometry({ slug, path, bevel, size }) {
  let g = cache.get(slug)
  if (g) return g
  const svg = `<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path d="${path}"/></svg>`
  const shapes = new SVGLoader().parse(svg).paths.flatMap((p) => toShapes(p)).map((s) => {
    const { shape, holes } = s.extractPoints(8)
    const out = new THREE.Shape(clean(shape))
    out.holes = holes.map((h) => new THREE.Path(clean(h)))
    return out
  })
  g = new THREE.ExtrudeGeometry(shapes, {
    depth: 2, curveSegments: 1, steps: 1, // already polygons
    bevelEnabled: true, bevelSegments: 3,
    bevelSize: size, bevelThickness: Math.max(0.12, size * 1.6),
    bevelOffset: bevel === 'in' ? -size : 0, // 'in' keeps the published silhouette
  })
  g = toCreasedNormals(g, Math.PI / 3) // in SVG units: it hashes positions on a 0.01 grid
  mergeGroups(g)                       // 2 draw calls instead of 2 per shape
  g.computeBoundingBox()
  cache.set(slug, g)
  return g
}

export const gold = new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial({ color: '#C89B3C', metalness: 1, roughness: 0.28 })

### 4.3 Dissolve swap (**patch compiled and rendered on r171 and r187; GL error 0**)

```js
const NOISE = /* glsl */`
float h31(vec3 p){ p = fract(p * 0.3183099 + 0.1); p *= 17.0; return fract(p.x * p.y * p.z * (p.x + p.y + p.z)); }
float vnoise(vec3 x){ vec3 i = floor(x), f = fract(x); f = f * f * (3.0 - 2.0 * f);
  return mix(mix(mix(h31(i), h31(i + vec3(1,0,0)), f.x), mix(h31(i + vec3(0,1,0)), h31(i + vec3(1,1,0)), f.x), f.y),
             mix(mix(h31(i + vec3(0,0,1)), h31(i + vec3(1,0,1)), f.x), mix(h31(i + vec3(0,1,1)), h31(i + vec3(1,1,1)), f.x), f.y), f.z); }`

export function addDissolve(material, u /* { uProgress:{value}, uEdge:{value:.08}, uEdgeColor:{value:Color('#0AC8B9')} } */) {
  material.onBeforeCompile = (shader) => {
    Object.assign(shader.uniforms, u)
    shader.vertexShader = shader.vertexShader
      .replace('#include <common>', '#include <common>\nvarying vec3 vObj;')
      .replace('#include <begin_vertex>', '#include <begin_vertex>\nvObj = position / 12.0;') // SVG units → ~[0,2]
    shader.fragmentShader = shader.fragmentShader
      .replace('#include <common>', `#include <common>\nvarying vec3 vObj;\nuniform float uProgress, uEdge;\nuniform vec3 uEdgeColor;\n${NOISE}`)
      .replace('#include <clipping_planes_fragment>', `#include <clipping_planes_fragment>
  float n = 0.75 * vnoise(vObj * 3.5) + 0.25 * vnoise(vObj * 9.0) + 0.15 * (vObj.y - 1.0); // SVG y is down: bottom burns first
  float cut = uProgress * 1.5 - 0.2;
  if (n < cut) discard;`)
      .replace('#include <emissivemap_fragment>', `#include <emissivemap_fragment>
  totalEmissiveRadiance += uEdgeColor * (1.0 - smoothstep(cut, cut + uEdge, n)) * step(0.001, uProgress) * 4.0;`)
  }
  material.customProgramCacheKey = () => 'dissolve'
  return material
}
```

How it behaves:
- **Noise is in object space**, so the pattern stays fixed while the logo swings.
- **Progress:** at 0.3 the bottom edge starts to burn, at 0.55 most of the logo is gone, at 0.8 it has completely vanished. Set `visible = false` once progress reaches 1.
- **The edge is HDR (×4)**, so the bloom in `webgl-magic.md` §5 picks it up.
- **Program sharing:** `onBeforeCompile` runs per material and `materialProperties.uniforms` is per material, so instances share one program but keep their own `uProgress` [src: WebGLRenderer `getProgram`].
- Discarded fragments show the background through the hole; the solid has no inner faces on `FrontSide`. That was accepted in the renders.

### 4.4 R3F component [proposal; uses only APIs present in fiber 8.18 / drei 9.122 / maath 0.10.8]

```jsx
// src/components/Stack/LogoChampion.jsx
import { useMemo, useRef } from 'react'
import { useFrame } from '@react-three/fiber'
import { easing } from 'maath'                     // dampE(current, target, smoothTime, delta) [src: maath d.ts]
import { useGSAP } from '@gsap/react'
import gsap from 'gsap'
import * as THREE from 'three'
import { logoGeometry, enamel, gold, addDissolve } from './logo3d'

export function LogoChampion({ spec, state, reduced, pointer, onGone }) {
  const geo = logoGeometry(spec)                                   // sync, cached
  const u = useMemo(() => ({ uProgress: { value: 1 }, uEdge: { value: 0.08 }, uEdgeColor: { value: new THREE.Color('#0AC8B9') } }), [])
  const mats = useMemo(() => [addDissolve(enamel(spec.color), u), addDissolve(gold.clone(), u)], [spec.slug])
  const rig = useRef(), swap = useRef()                            // rig: idle + hover; swap: GSAP only (never both on one property)
  const c = geo.boundingBox.getCenter(new THREE.Vector3())
  const k = (2 / 24) * spec.optical

  useGSAP(() => {
    if (reduced) { u.uProgress.value = state === 'in' ? 0 : 1; if (state === 'out') onGone(); return }
    const L = 0.6                                                  // client base unit (champ-select.md §6.3)
    if (state === 'in') gsap.timeline()
      .fromTo(u.uProgress, { value: 1 }, { value: 0, duration: L, ease: 'power2.out' }, 0.25 * L)
      .fromTo(swap.current.rotation, { y: -0.8 }, { y: 0, duration: 1.5 * L, ease: 'expo.out' }, 0.25 * L)
      .fromTo(swap.current.scale, { x: 0.9, y: 0.9, z: 0.9 }, { x: 1, y: 1, z: 1, duration: L, ease: 'back.out(1.6)' }, 0.25 * L)
    else gsap.timeline({ onComplete: onGone })
      .to(u.uProgress, { value: 1, duration: 0.5 * L, ease: 'power2.in' }, 0)
      .to(swap.current.rotation, { y: '+=0.6', duration: 0.5 * L, ease: 'power2.in' }, 0)
  }, { dependencies: [state, reduced] })

  useFrame((s, dt) => {
    if (reduced) return
    const t = s.clock.elapsedTime
    rig.current.position.y = 0.06 * Math.sin(1.1 * t)             // idle float (champ-select.md §5.5)
    easing.dampE(rig.current.rotation,
      [-pointer.current.y * 0.2, 0.52 * Math.sin(0.5 * t) + pointer.current.x * 0.25, 0], 0.25, dt) // ±30° swing + ≤14° hover tilt
  })

  return (
    <group ref={rig}>
      <group ref={swap}>
        <mesh geometry={geo} material={mats} scale={[k, -k, k]} position={[-c.x * k, c.y * k, -c.z * k]} />
      </group>
    </group>
  )
}
```

Usage notes:
- **Stage slots:** keep `[{ id, slug, state }]`. On a new selection, mark the current slot `'out'` and append `'in'`; `onGone` removes the slot. Selecting quickly A → B → A works because slots are keyed by an incrementing `id`.
- **Pointer:** on the fixed full-screen canvas from `webgl-magic.md`, track a window `pointermove` and normalise it to the stage's `getBoundingClientRect()`. Use 0 when the pointer is outside, so `dampE` eases back.
- **Clock:** under the single clock in `webgl-magic.md` §2.3 nothing else is needed. With `frameloop="demand"`, call `invalidate()` from the timeline's `onUpdate`.
- **Accessibility:** the canvas is decorative. Selection happens through the DOM grid buttons (keyboard and screen readers); the 3D only mirrors the state.

### 5.3 Shadow [src]

- **Avoid drei `ContactShadows` here.** With its defaults (`frames = Infinity`, `resolution = 512`, `smooth = true`) it re-renders the **whole scene** with a depth override material every frame, plus 4 blur passes (2 more because `smooth` is on). On the single shared canvas that means the hero core and particles too.
- **Keep the blob instead.** The repo's `FloatingIcon.tsx` already maps float height to shadow scale and opacity. Make it a radial-gradient plane with radius 0.55 × logo width on the pedestal top.
- Use `ContactShadows` only on the HIGH tier, with `frames={1}` while the logo is idle-paused.

## 8. Corrections to earlier docs

- **`webgl-magic.md` §9:**
  - "`flutter.svg` has no `<path>` and three gradients" is wrong. It has **5 `<polygon>`s and 1 gradient**, a shadow overlay at fill-opacity .8. `kotlin.svg` is the file with 3 gradients.
  - Its `logoGeometry()` uses `SVGLoader.createShapes`, which is deprecated in r185+ (it warns on every call) under the r187 upgrade that doc recommends. Use the §3.1 shim.
  - Its default outward bevel (bevelSize .27) widens the silhouette and fuses PostgreSQL's gaps (rOut .024).
  - It keeps flat normals, so the gold bands, and it bakes transforms.
  - The triangle counts there (React 18,682) are for curveSegments 12 unsanitized. The recommended pipeline gives 10,892.
- **`champ-select.md` §5.5:**
  - "ExtrudeGeometry puts caps in group 0 and walls in group 1" is true **per shape**, so material arrays cost 2 draw calls per shape until `mergeGroups` (§3.5).
  - "`scale.y = -1`" is correct on the mesh but wrong if baked into the geometry (§3.6).
  - "Angular: `angular.svg` shield halves" is the 2016 logo that Angular's press kit says to avoid.
  - "curveSegments 8 for paths over 10 kB" helps less than sanitizing: NestJS 37k → 22k triangles.
  - "Kotlin, Supabase, Python, Flutter: SVG gradients come through as `url(#…)`" no longer holds from r185, where gradients work through `createFillMaterial` (with the offset bug in §3.8).
- **`webgl-magic.md` §0.6 and §5:** AgX as the last tone mapping step desaturates brand colours (§3.7). Use NEUTRAL, at least for the stage.

## 9. Method (reproducible)

- **Machine:** Windows 11, Intel i5-10210U (4C/8T), Intel UHD + GeForce MX130.
- **Browser:** Chromium 152 in the Claude browser pane. The tab was often hidden, so browser timings are noisy; the geometry timings in this report come from Node v24.18.1 (V8) running the same three code.

- **Simple Icons:** `simple-icons@16.37.0` data and index extracted with `npm pack`.
- **gilbarbara files:** `@iconify-json/logos@1.2.19`.
- **Scripts** (scratch folder `C:/Users/abdel/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/8d57b08e2422bb31/1bd66213-b433-46ef-8926-dbe5a9999d6c/scratchpad/l3d/work/`):
  - `svg-audit.mjs`: structure audit
  - `lib.mjs` and `measure.mjs`: parse, shape counts, extrusion timing, polygon bevel test

  - `render.mjs`, `final.mjs`, `grad.mjs`, `dissolve.mjs`: renders saved as PNG under `results/`
- **Raster topology test:** the path is filled at 40 px/unit with 90 px padding.
